# What Aymora says it is (source notes)

Sources: Aymora's LinkedIn About page, 22 pages of their LinkedIn posts (saved as PDF),
and the waitlist site at aymora.vercel.app. Everything below is from those, not guesswork.

## Product promises
- AI matchmaker, "not another swipe deck". Tagline: "Meet the people you should've met".
- Waitlist copy: "At least 1 guaranteed match every week", "No quality compromise",
  "AI matchmaking for India's 200M+ daters".
- Flow: short questionnaire -> personality profile -> "one intentional introduction at a time",
  "introduced when it's mutual".
- Four steps in their own graphic: You tell us who you are -> Our AI learns what matters ->
  AI matches, not randomly -> You get quality over quantity ("a small number of high-quality matches").
- Principles: privacy by default, conversation before judgment, "apps profit when you keep
  swiping, we win when you stop, because it worked".
- Early access: free Aymora Plus for the first 1,000 on the waitlist.

## Their own survey (the numbers I use as default engine weights)
On-ground survey by Aymora, 2,354 daters across 12 cities in India:
- 73% Vibes and personality
- 64% Communication and understanding
- 48% Shared values and interests
- 42% Sense of humor and positivity
- 27% Physical appearance

## Earlier campus positioning
- College-exclusive: verified college email, private mutual interest, crushes stay anonymous
  unless both people choose each other. "Our first release is intentionally simple."
- Small, closed pools per campus. This is the hardest case for any "guaranteed match" promise.

## Team and voice
- Founder: Nikunj Rai Juneja. Company founded 2026, 2 to 10 employees.
- Hiring a Technical Founding Member: modern web apps, Flutter mobile apps, backend APIs and
  databases, end-to-end product features.
- Voice: playful, pop-culture, humor as a compatibility signal ("moving the podium half an inch
  funny"), "be curious, not judgmental".

## The gap this prototype targets
"One guaranteed match every week" is the hardest engineering promise in the deck. Ranking
candidates per user does not keep it: the same popular people get proposed to many users, and
a thin or lopsided pool leaves people with nothing. Keeping the promise needs a pool-wide
weekly assignment, a fairness rule for people who missed out, an honest quality floor, and
a way to tell the team where the pool is too thin. That is what Weekly Drop builds.
